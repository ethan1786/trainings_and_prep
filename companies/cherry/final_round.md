# Final Round — CTO + Leadership (75 min)

**Format:** Career narrative walkthrough with CTO (Charles, Co-Founder) + leadership team members
**Not technical** — this is about you as a person, your journey, and your impact

---

## What They're Evaluating

From the interview doc: "your journey... what's motivated your choices, what you've learned, what you're proud of, and how you approach new challenges. Clear, thoughtful communication and genuine reflection."

They explicitly want: "objective data points & quantified impact."

---

## Your Career Story — Chapter by Chapter

Practice telling this as a coherent 10-15 minute narrative. Each chapter should answer: what did you do, why did you choose it, what did you learn, what impact did you have?

### Chapter 1: Education (1 min)
- B.S. in Information Science (Beijing) → M.S. in ECE (Northeastern, Boston)
- Came to the US for grad school, stayed to build a career in software

### Chapter 2: MathWorks — First Job (1 min)
- Java backend for MATLAB/Simulink tooling
- Learned engineering rigor — testing, automation, build systems
- "This taught me the fundamentals, but I wanted to be closer to the product and the customer."

### Chapter 3: Toast — Early-Stage Builder (2-3 min)
- Joined during hypergrowth — among the earliest engineers
- Built core services end-to-end: Order Service, Labor Service, admin dashboards, Android app
- Set up AWS infrastructure from scratch, built custom API gateway
- **Impact:** These services became the foundation for Toast's platform as it scaled from startup to public company
- **Why I left:** "I'd built the foundation, and the company was scaling into specialized teams. I wanted to stay generalist."

### Chapter 4: Applause — Full-Stack Technical Lead (2-3 min)
- Full-stack across everything: Angular + Node.js BFF + backend + infrastructure
- Led auth modernization (Keycloak SSO, Kong API gateway, custom Python plugins)
- Set up service mesh (Linkerd + K8s), drove event sourcing architecture
- **Impact:** Unified auth across 3 platforms, modernized the entire API infrastructure layer
- **Why I left:** "The company was shrinking. I wanted a bigger stage with harder technical problems."

### Chapter 5: CarGurus — Principal / Tech Lead (3-4 min)
- Led the biggest technical initiative: redesign of the highest-traffic API
- Partnered with 10 teams across 3 departments
- **Impact:** p99 250ms → 50ms (5x), availability 98% → 99.9%, deploy time 1hr → 3min (20x)
- Built GraphQL federation, digital commerce platform, established org-wide architecture standards
- Mentored senior engineers toward Principal scope
- **Why I'm looking:** "I've been here 6+ years and delivered the most impactful projects of my career. I'm looking for the next challenge — a place where I can have that same kind of outsized impact on a smaller, faster-moving team."

### Chapter 6: Why Cherry (1-2 min)
- "I've been a generalist my entire career — frontend, backend, infrastructure. Cherry needs someone who can own the full stack."
- "I was an early engineer at Toast and saw what it takes to build a platform from the ground up. I want to do that again."
- "Fintech is a domain where engineering quality directly impacts the business — payments, compliance, trust. That's the kind of work I find most meaningful."

## Likely Questions

**"Tell me about a time you failed or made a mistake."**
- Pick something real. Show what you learned. Don't be defensive.
- Example: a rollout that went wrong, a design decision you'd reverse, a collaboration that broke down.

**"What motivates you?"**
- Building things end-to-end. Seeing your work in production. Working with a small team where your contribution is visible.

**"Why are you leaving CarGurus?"**
- Growth plateau. You've delivered the biggest project. Looking for early-stage impact again.
- Do NOT mention job searching concerns, market anxiety, or compensation.

**"What are you looking for in your next role?"**
- Full-stack ownership, small team, meaningful product, visible impact
- "I want to be the person who builds the thing, not the person who reviews the doc about the thing."

**"Where do you see yourself in 3-5 years?"**
- Growing with the company. Taking on more scope as the team and product grow. Eventually shaping the engineering org.

---

## Tone

This is the most personal round. They want to like you as a person, not just respect you as an engineer. Be genuine. Show enthusiasm for what you've built. Be honest about what you want. Don't be robotic or over-rehearsed — have the story in your head but let it flow naturally.

Charles is a co-founder and CTO — he's evaluating culture fit and long-term alignment. Show that you care about building something meaningful, not just collecting a paycheck.
