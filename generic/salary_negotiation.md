# Salary Negotiation Playbook

> For Staff/Principal-level roles at top-tier companies (Stripe, Netflix, Datadog, Coinbase, Airbnb, Figma, Circle).
> Target TC range: $350K–$900K depending on company and level.

---

## Golden Rules

1. **Never name a number first.** Let them anchor. If pressed, say "I'm focused on finding the right fit — I'm confident we can find a number that works for both sides."
2. **Never accept the first offer.** Every first offer has room. Even a polite counter gets 5-15% more.
3. **Compete offers against each other.** The single most powerful negotiation tool. Time your processes to overlap.
4. **Negotiate after the "yes."** Once they've decided to hire you, leverage shifts entirely to you. Don't negotiate during the interview.
5. **Everything is negotiable.** Base, equity, sign-on, vesting schedule, start date, PTO, remote flexibility, title, relevel.

---

## Phase 1: Recruiter Screen — Deflect Comp Questions

### If asked "What are your compensation expectations?"

**Best response:**
> "I'm really focused on finding the right role and team fit right now. I know [Company] is competitive at the Staff/Principal level, and I'm confident we can find a number that works for both sides once we get further along. Can you share the range for this role?"

**If they insist:**
> "My current total comp is competitive for a principal-level engineer in the Boston market, and I'm looking for something in line with the top of the market for this level. I'd love to learn more about the role first before getting into specifics."

**If they share the range:** Thank them, note it, and say "That's helpful context. Let's continue the conversation."

### If asked "What's your current compensation?"

> "I'd prefer to focus on the value I'd bring to this role rather than anchor on my current comp. What's the range you're working with for this position?"

(Note: In Massachusetts, it's illegal for employers to ask your salary history before making an offer.)

---

## Phase 2: During Interviews — Build Leverage

- **Perform exceptionally.** The better you interview, the more leverage you have. Strong performance = higher initial offer + more room to negotiate.
- **Signal options without being explicit.** If it comes up naturally: "I'm in conversations with a few companies" or "I'm exploring a few opportunities."
- **Don't reveal where.** If asked which companies: "I'd rather not share specifics at this stage, but I'm in active processes at similar-stage companies in similar roles."
- **Time your processes.** Try to get offers within the same 1-2 week window. If one company is faster, ask others if they can accelerate.

### Requesting timeline acceleration:
> "I've received an offer from another company with a deadline of [date]. I'm very interested in [your company] and would love to complete the process before then. Is there any way to accelerate the remaining steps?"

---

## Phase 3: The Offer — Evaluate Before Responding

### When you receive an offer:

1. **Thank them enthusiastically.** "I'm really excited about this opportunity. Thank you for the offer."
2. **Don't react to the number.** Don't say "that's great" or "that's low." Just thank them.
3. **Ask for it in writing.** "Could you send the full offer details in writing so I can review them carefully?"
4. **Buy time.** "I'd like to take a few days to review everything thoroughly. When do you need a response by?"
5. **Standard deadline:** Ask for 1 week. Most companies give 3-5 business days but will extend if asked.

### Offer evaluation checklist:

| Component | What to look at |
|---|---|
| **Base salary** | Compare to Levels.fyi for role/level/company |
| **Equity** | RSUs vs. options; vesting schedule (4yr/1yr cliff is standard); refresh grants |
| **Sign-on bonus** | One-time cash; sometimes negotiable to 2-year payout |
| **Target bonus** | Percentage of base; how guaranteed vs. performance-dependent |
| **Total comp (TC)** | Base + equity/yr + bonus; this is the number that matters |
| **Level** | Confirm the level matches expectations; affects future promotions and comp bands |
| **Vesting schedule** | Netflix: all-cash, no vesting. Others: typically 4yr/1yr cliff. Some offer accelerated vesting. |
| **Remote policy** | Fully remote? Hybrid? How many days? Is it policy or preference? |
| **PTO** | Unlimited (verify culture) vs. defined days |
| **Start date** | Flexibility here; can negotiate 2-4 weeks buffer |

## Phase 4: Counter-Offer — Templates

### Scenario A: You have one offer, no competing offers

> "Thank you again for the offer — I'm genuinely excited about [Company] and this role. After reviewing the details, I'd like to discuss the compensation. Based on my research and conversations with peers at similar levels, I believe the total comp for this role at top-tier companies is closer to [X]. Given my experience — 13 years, including [specific high-impact work relevant to them] — I'd like to explore whether we can close the gap. I'm thinking [specific number] for base and [specific equity number] would better reflect the value I'd bring."

### Scenario B: You have a competing offer

> "Thank you for the offer — I'm very excited about [Company]. I want to be transparent: I've received a competing offer from [another company / a comparable company] at [X total comp]. I prefer [Company] for [specific reason — team, mission, role scope], but the comp gap is significant. Is there room to match or close the gap? I'd love to make this work."

### Scenario C: You want to negotiate equity specifically

> "The base salary is reasonable, but I'd like to discuss the equity component. Given the growth trajectory of [Company] and my commitment to a multi-year tenure, I'd like to request [X additional RSUs / a higher grant]. I'm also interested in discussing the refresh grant policy — how are annual refreshes determined?"

### Scenario D: You want to negotiate level

> "Based on our conversations during the interview process, particularly around [specific scope/leadership expectations discussed], I believe this role aligns more closely with [higher level] in terms of scope and impact. My experience leading [specific example] and setting architectural direction across [X teams] maps to the expectations I've seen for [higher level]. Would you be open to reconsidering the level?"

---

## Phase 5: Closing — Getting to Yes

### If they say "this is our best offer":
> "I understand there may be constraints. Are there other components we can look at — sign-on bonus, equity acceleration, a performance review at 6 months for a comp adjustment, or remote work flexibility?"

### If they push for a decision:
> "I'm very close to a decision. I want to make sure I'm making the right choice for both of us. Can I have until [specific date — 2-3 more days]?"

### Accepting:
> "I'm thrilled to accept. Thank you for working with me on the compensation. I'm looking forward to [starting / joining the team / contributing to X]. I'll look for the final offer letter and plan for a [start date] start."

## Netflix-Specific Notes

Netflix compensation works differently:
- **All cash.** No RSUs, no vesting. You choose your cash/stock allocation annually.
- **No bonuses.** Everything is in the salary number.
- **"Top of market" philosophy.** They benchmark against the highest offer you'd get elsewhere.
- **Annual re-benchmarking.** Your comp is adjusted each year based on market data.
- **Negotiation lever:** If you have a competing offer with high equity, Netflix will often raise their cash number to match the expected TC. Explicitly sharing competing offer TC is effective here.

## Timing Strategy

**Ideal scenario:** Have 2-3 offers within the same 1-2 week window.

- Apply to top choices (Stripe, Netflix) first — they often have longer processes
- Apply to faster-moving companies (Coinbase, Circle) slightly later
- When the first offer arrives, use it to accelerate others
- When multiple offers arrive, negotiate simultaneously
- Close within 1-2 weeks of first offer to avoid losing any

**If processes are misaligned:**
- Ask faster companies for more time: "I'm finalizing another process and want to give your offer the consideration it deserves. Can I have until [date]?"
- Ask slower companies to accelerate: "I've received an offer with a deadline. I'm very interested in your role — is there any way to move the timeline up?"
